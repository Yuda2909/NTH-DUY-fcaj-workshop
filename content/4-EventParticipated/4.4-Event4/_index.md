# Agentic AI Build Week

## Event Objectives

- Showcase how different teams approached Agentic AI product building in real scenarios.
- Learn how to turn messy requirements into usable architecture, workflows, and demos.
- Compare four different project directions: solution architecture automation, conversational ordering, hackathon prototyping, and signal intelligence for business decision support.
- Capture practical lessons about building, testing, presenting, and improving AI products.

---

## Team / Project Lineup

- **Plan V** - Solution Architect Professional Native App
- **One Team** - AI-Powered Conversation Ordering
- **3KA** - Hackathon Journey / S.H.E.P.H.E.R.D.
- **Signal Scout** - Evidence-based business signal intelligence

---

## Key Highlights

### 1. From Requirements to Architecture

The **Solution Architect Professional Native App** team showed how natural-language requirements can be transformed into a more structured design workflow.

Their demo focused on helping solution architects:

- Read project requirements faster.
- Draft an initial architecture.
- Generate editable diagrams.
- Produce directional AWS cost estimates.

The most useful idea was that the tool does not replace the architect. Instead, it gives a grounded first draft that can be reviewed, corrected, and refined much faster than starting from a blank page.

### 2. Conversation as the Product Interface

The **One Team** presentation showed a conversational ordering agent built for channels such as Zalo and Messenger.

The project emphasized that ordering in chat is not just a chatbot problem. It is a system problem that must handle:

- Product selection.
- Quantity updates.
- Variants and business rules.
- Cart verification.
- A smooth flow from intent to confirmed order.

Their message was clear: a good agent should act on real business rules, not just reply nicely.

### 3. What Hackathon Building Really Feels Like

The **3KA** team shared the journey behind **S.H.E.P.H.E.R.D.**, a crowd-monitoring and hazard-response concept built during a 24-hour hackathon.

Their story covered:

- The pressure of building under time limits.
- How the idea evolved during the event.
- What they learned from real-time computer vision and agentic workflows.
- The biggest challenges around latency, tracking, scope, and reliability.

This presentation was especially valuable because it showed the human side of building: confusion, fatigue, debugging, teamwork, and the satisfaction of getting something working in time.

### 4. Connecting Scattered Signals Into a Clear Story

The **Signal Scout** team presented an AI system designed to detect corporate strategic changes early by collecting, validating, and analyzing evidence from multiple sources.

Their value proposition focused on helping enterprise strategy, competitive intelligence, and risk teams:

- Connect scattered signals into one clear story.
- Build scenarios from financial and operational metrics.
- Surface timelines and risk alerts.
- Support maintain, adapt, or accelerate decisions with transparent evidence.

The architecture and cost slide also showed that they were thinking about real product constraints, not just the AI layer. That made the project feel more practical and production-minded.

## What I Learned

### Agentic AI Needs More Than a Prompt

The event reinforced that Agentic AI is not only about prompting a model. It also needs:

- A clear workflow.
- Tool usage.
- Verification steps.
- Practical guardrails around real business actions.

### Good Demos Come From Clear Scope

Each team had a different scope, but all of them benefited from focusing on one sharp idea instead of trying to do everything at once.

That made their demos easier to understand and more believable.

### Building in a Team Teaches More Than Building Alone

The hackathon journey showed that teamwork, deadlines, and presentation pressure expose gaps that solo practice often hides.

That is also where the strongest lessons appear: scope control, role clarity, fast iteration, and learning to explain your product simply.

### Evidence Matters in AI Decision Support

Signal Scout added another important lesson: when AI is used for business decision support, the system has to be transparent and verifiable.

It is not enough to generate an answer. The product also needs evidence collection, analysis rules, and a clear way to explain why the recommendation exists.
